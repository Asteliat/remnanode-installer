Packet loss

Last edited 3 days ago · 14 revisions · reviewed
Contents 1 Causes · 2 Measuring · 3 Mitigation · 4 See also

Packet loss is the fraction of packets sent across a network that never reach their destination. It is usually expressed as a percentage and measured over a window of seconds to minutes.[1]

1 Causes

The most common causes are congestion at a bottleneck, faulty cabling, overloaded queues on a router, and wireless interference. Loss that appears only under load usually points to a buffer that is too small rather than to a broken link.

Rule of thumb: a flat 0.1–1% loss on a long path is often acceptable for bulk transfer, but it is noticeable in real-time voice.

2 Measuring

Counters are read from interface statistics — rx_dropped, tx_dropped and the per-queue drops. Compare them with the total packet count over the same interval; a single spike right after a reboot is expected.

3 Mitigation

Increase queue depth, enable a modern congestion control algorithm, and avoid oversubscribing uplinks. Where loss comes from the last mile, nothing on the server side will fix it.
